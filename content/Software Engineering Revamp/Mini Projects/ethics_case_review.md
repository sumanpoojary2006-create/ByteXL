## Background

A fintech start-up's engineers are asked to add a feature to a lending app: reading the borrower's contact list to nudge friends when a payment is late. It will raise repayments. It will also expose people who never agreed to anything. The engineers are junior, the deadline is close, and the request comes from the founder.

Professional practice is what you do when a legitimate instruction collides with a duty to other people. This project asks you to analyse such a case in a structured way, rather than by instinct.

## What You Will Build

A case review of the lending app request: affected parties, obligations, options, a recommendation and the words you would actually say to the founder.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this project, you will be able to:
- Identify everyone affected by a technical decision, including those absent from the room
- Relate the case to professional codes of conduct and privacy law principles
- Generate alternatives that meet the goal without the harm
- Communicate a refusal or concern constructively

**Difficulty:** Beginner · **Estimated time:** 2 hours

## Starting Material

| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Request | When a payment is more than 7 days late, message up to five contacts from the borrower's phone |
| Goal | Reduce late repayments, currently 18 per cent |
| Consent | The borrower accepted a general permission to "improve services" |
| Contacts | Have not consented to anything |
| Pressure | The investor review is in three weeks |

This legal and ethical analysis is for learning. Real products need advice from a qualified professional.

## Tasks

### Task 1: Who Is Affected

1. List at least five affected parties, including the borrower, the contacts and the engineers themselves.
2. For each, describe the benefit or harm in one sentence.

### Task 2: Obligations

1. State two principles from a professional code of conduct that apply and explain why.
2. State two data protection principles, such as purpose limitation and data minimisation, that apply and explain why.
3. Say whether the borrower's general consent is enough, and why.

### Task 3: Options

1. Write at least three alternatives that pursue the same goal, such as reminders to the borrower, a repayment plan or an opt-in trusted contact.
2. Compare them in a table with the columns Option, Effect on the goal, Harm risk and Legal risk.

### Task 4: Respond

1. Write what you would say to the founder in five sentences or fewer, offering a better option instead of only objecting.
2. Describe what you would do if the request is repeated and refused is not accepted, including who inside the company you would go to.

**Answer these questions after completing all tasks:**
- Why do the contacts matter even though they are not users?
- At what point does following instructions stop being a defence?
- What record would you keep of this conversation, and why?
